# Full-Bleed Image Grid

## The idea
Most layouts treat an image as decoration behind the content. This one inverts that: the image *is* the stage, filling most or all of the viewport, and the content sits on top as a small number of deliberately anchored overlay blocks — not a stack of marketing sections. The grid exists to impose order on the image, not to compete with it.

## Making the image carry the page
- Let the image or video fill the full viewport as a foundation layer, never shrunk to a card or thumbnail.
- Add a directional gradient or tonal vignette (not a flat dark overlay) so text stays legible while the image still reads as present and expansive.
- Anchor the primary content low or along one deliberate edge, rather than dead-center — centering content on top of a big image tends to crowd it and flatten the composition.
- Keep the palette restrained: neutrals, off-whites, smoke grays, and at most one accent pulled from the image itself.

## The grid sits quietly on top
- Add visible-but-quiet structure over the image: outer rails, a center guide, small corner markers — just enough to feel intentional, not enough to distract from the photography.
- Split the anchored content into two lanes: one for the headline + primary action, a separate lane for a supporting element (a quote, a metric, a short proof point).
- Supporting cards should read as lightly framed and low-fill/transparent, so they rest on the image instead of fighting it with heavy container chrome.
- Optional atmosphere: faint animated line fields, additive-blend streaks, or slow parallax drift — used to add a technical, premium feel, not as the focal point.

## Typography and motion
Headlines stay confident and clean — a strong sans-serif, not decorative. Body copy stays soft and low-contrast so it doesn't compete with the image. Motion should read as reveal and drift (masked text entrances, slow line movement, gentle hover sweeps) rather than busy interaction — this is a cinematic register, not a playful one.

## Tuning knobs
- **Image dominance** — decide how much of the experience the media occupies first, then fit content density around that, not the reverse.
- **Overlay strength** — increase gradient/vignette until text is reliably legible, but stop before the image starts reading as flat or washed out.
- **Grid visibility** — rails and markers should feel like structure, not decoration; if they're the first thing noticed, they're too strong.
- **Atmosphere** — add or drop the animated line/glow layer depending on whether the page should feel more like a product or more purely cinematic.
- **Anchor position** — shift the content lanes left, right, or lower to preserve negative space; this is the main lever for making the composition feel intentional rather than centered-by-default.

## What breaks this pattern
- Shrinking the image back down to a background behind a conventional centered layout — that's a different (weaker) pattern wearing this one's clothes.
- Overfilling the viewport with cards and text until the image-first premise disappears.
- Bright, multi-color accents, which puncture the calm/cinematic tone this pattern depends on.
- Heavy solid-fill containers that sever the visual relationship between overlay and image.
- Making the grid invisible entirely — the structure should be felt even when not consciously noticed.
